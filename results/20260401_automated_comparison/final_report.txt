SECTION 1: INTRODUCTION
EEG microstates represent quasi-stable topographic patterns that reflect the discrete
functional states of the brain. Clustering agreement is critical for ensuring that
neural signatures are consistent across different algorithmic frameworks. This work
evaluates inter-algorithm consistency in both raw sensor and latent domains.

SECTION 2: METHODOLOGY
Algorithms: KMeans, AAHC, TAAHC, PCA, ICA (clustering integration).
Latent Projections: PCA, ICA mappings.
Metrics: Adjusted Rand Index (ARI) and Normalized Mutual Information (NMI).
Optimization: GFP-based extraction was utilized for computational efficiency.

SECTION 3: EXPERIMENTS
Parameters: n_states [4, 5, 6], Latent Methods [PCA, ICA].
Design: Pairwise agreement computed across Raw and Latent spaces for each subject.

SECTION 5: KEY FINDINGS
1. Latent space transformation significantly alters algorithm agreement trends.
2. PCA emerged as the most consistent latent representation.
3. Optimal agreement observed at n_states = 5.

SECTION 6: TOPO MAP SIMILARITY ANALYSIS
In addition to label-based agreement, spatial similarity of the extracted microstate
topographies was evaluated using the absolute Pearson correlation coefficient. Because
the ordering of clusters is arbitrary across algorithms, the Hungarian algorithm
(linear sum assignment) was utilized to find the optimal one-to-one mapping between
topomaps that maximizes spatial correlation.
Results indicate spatial consistency varies across cluster sizes (k=4-8) and between
raw and latent domains. Detailed trends are visualized in the generated plots.

SECTION 7: COMBINED INSIGHTS
A critical observation is the relationship between label agreement (ARI/NMI) and spatial
map similarity. High map similarity implies algorithms identify the same neural generators,
but does not guarantee identical temporal labeling due to boundary sensitivities. Conversely,
differences highlight algorithmic biases in boundary estimation despite spatial alignment.

SECTION 8: EXTENDED NOVELTY
This work contributes:
1. Inter-algorithm agreement analysis using robust metrics (ARI/NMI).
2. Spatial topomap similarity quantification using distance-matched Pearson correlation.
3. Hungarian algorithm integration to resolve the microstate label permutation problem.
4. Multi-scale evaluation across an expanded range of cluster sizes (k=4 to 8).
5. Dual-level comparison (label vs. spatial) across raw sensor and latent domains.

SECTION 9: CONCLUSION
The results demonstrate that latent representations can enhance or degrade algorithm
consistency depending on the projection method.
